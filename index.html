How to play

Four words are jumbled together in one grid. Change the tile colors until every color spells a real word.

1Read one color at a time

To see what a color spells, read only the tiles of that color, left to right and top to bottom, like reading a page. Here the four coral tiles, read in order, spell SPIN.

The four boxes under the grid show what each color spells right now. The puzzle starts with the colors mixed up, so at first they show nonsense. You have solved it when all four show real words.

2Tiles are linked in groups of four

Every tile belongs to a group of four, called a pinwheel. Here the dotted line joins one of them.

Each pinwheel always holds one tile of each color: one coral, one yellow, one green and one blue. So every pinwheel gives exactly one letter to each of the four words.

After

Tap any tile to outline its pinwheel. Nothing changes yet, and the letter it controls in each word lights up in the boxes under the grid.

Tap the same pinwheel again and its four colors each move one tile clockwise. It still has one of each color, just in different places.

The letters never move. Only the colors change, so one turn changes one letter in all four words at once.

Keep turning pinwheels until all four boxes show real words. There is only one solution, and all four words fall into place at the same moment.

4Striped tiles are already correct

SWEY

A striped tile already has its correct color and will not change. The four corners always start striped, so the top-left letter is always the first letter of its word.

Controls

  • Hint sets one pinwheel for you and stripes it.
  • Undo takes back your last turn.
  • Reset puts the colors back where they started.
Tips
  • Start with the theme. The four words share a theme, shown above the grid. Guess a word that fits it, then look for its letters in reading order.
  • Use the corners. The top-left letter starts the coral word and the bottom-right letter ends the green word. They never change.
  • Top rows are beginnings, bottom rows are endings. A letter in the top row is near the start of its word. One in the bottom row is near the end.

An actual secret code. Pinwheel comes from the Turning Grille, a cipher used to hide messages for hundreds of years. A card with holes was laid over a grid of letters. The holes showed part of the message, and each quarter turn of the card showed more.
